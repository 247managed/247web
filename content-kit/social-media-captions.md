## Thought Leadership

### Post 7: Hiring vs Outsourcing Engineering

**LinkedIn**:
```
"Should we hire or outsource this development work?"

It's the wrong question. Better framing: "What type of capacity do we need, and for how long?"

Hire when:
• The work is ongoing and central to competitive advantage  
• You need institutional knowledge embedded permanently
• Team culture and collaboration are critical success factors
• You're building long-term internal engineering capacity

Outsource when:
• You need senior skills immediately (can't wait 3-6 months for hiring)
• The project has a defined scope and end date
• You want to test a technology direction before committing internally
• Your roadmap is blocked by capacity, not strategy

Both models have merit. The mistake is treating "build a team" as the only respectable option or treating "outsource" as inherently compromised.

247 Managed Network Solutions works with companies who need senior engineering capacity without the 6-month ramp time of hiring. We're not a permanent replacement for internal teams - we're capacity for defined initiatives, rescue projects, or managed care of existing systems.

Different tool for different jobs.

#outsourcing #softwaredevelopment #hiring #engineering #businessstrategy
```

### Post 8: When Projects Stall

**LinkedIn**:
```
Common patterns we see when projects stall:

→ **Technical decisions deferred**: "We'll figure out deployment later" becomes a blocker at 80% complete.

→ **Scope uncertainty**: No one can define "done," so work continues without direction.

→ **Key person dependency**: One developer holds all the knowledge; they leave or get stretched thin.

→ **Integration surprises**: The external API doesn't work as documented. Now what?

→ **Quality accumulation**: "We'll fix the bugs later" until 'later' becomes a crisis.

Project rescue isn't about working faster. It's about:
1. Making the next step visible (even if it's hard)
2. Reducing dependencies and unknowns
3. Establishing what "shippable" actually means  
4. Building confidence through small, verifiable progress

We've recovered dozens of stalled projects. The common element: someone needed to make hard decisions, cut scope, and ship something useful.

If your project has been "almost done" for 3+ months, that's a signal.

#projectmanagement #softwaredevelopment #projectrescue #engineering
```

## Behind the Scenes

### Post 9: Our Process

**LinkedIn**:
```
Our four-phase delivery process:

**Clarify** | 1-2 weeks
Map goals, users, constraints, systems. Define the smallest useful release. Exit criteria: everyone agrees on what we're building and why.

**Plan** | 1-2 weeks  
Shape backlog, define risks, confirm architecture, set delivery cadence. Exit criteria: roadmap is prioritized, risks are visible, team knows what's next.

**Build** | Variable (2-12+ weeks)
Implement in focused increments with reviewable code and steady communication. Exit criteria: features are complete, tested, and demonstrable.

**Stabilize** | 1-2 weeks
Test, harden, document, deploy, support handoff. Exit criteria: application is in production with confidence.

This isn't agile theater or waterfall disguised - it's pragmatic delivery with clear checkpoints.

We adapt the timeline to your constraints, but we don't skip phases. Clarifying requirements saves weeks of rework. Planning architecture prevents integration crises. Building incrementally means you see progress. Stabilizing properly means production doesn't become a panic.

Simple process. Executed well.

#softwaredevelopment #projectmanagement #agile #delivery
```
